## 1. For the solicitor

ANCHOR is governance, trust, learning, intelligence, and readiness infrastructure for responsible AI use in veterinary clinics. It is not a clinical AI tool: it performs no diagnosis, prescribing, treatment planning, or triage, and stores no clinical records — its evidence layer is metadata-only (identifiers, hashes, timestamps, statuses, counts). Human review before operational use is a product-enforced posture, not a marketing line.

Our claim boundary is fixed: ANCHOR is *aligned with emerging professional expectations*; it is not, and will not be described as, RCVS-approved, framework-compliant, certified, or a compliance guarantee. Learning features are described only as CPD-recordable AI literacy activity — not certified CPD, not proof of competence.

Questions we expect to bring you when the final RCVS / VATA output is available: (a) whether any provider-facing expectations could be read to cover governance software like ANCHOR; (b) how far marketing may reference the framework by name; (c) retention alignment between our metadata evidence and any record expectations; (d) boundaries for any client-consent-adjacent wording; (e) liability wording if practices keep vendor-diligence records inside ANCHOR.

## 2. For a practice owner

ANCHOR gives your practice a working answer to "how do we use AI responsibly — and how would we show it?" It holds your AI-use policy and records that staff have read it; provides CPD-recordable AI literacy modules for the team; keeps a metadata-only receipt trail showing AI-assisted drafts were reviewed by a person before use; helps you publish a clear client-facing statement about how the practice uses AI; and gives you a place to log near-misses so problems become learning rather than folklore.

It does not diagnose, prescribe, or make clinical decisions — and it never will. It does not store your clinical records or client conversations; it keeps the governance evidence *about* AI use, not the content. Professional judgement stays yours; ANCHOR makes the governance around it visible. It will not make you "compliant" with anything — no software can — but it helps you evidence responsible practice aligned with the direction professional expectations are moving.

## 3. For a corporate group / multi-site operator

ANCHOR standardises AI governance across sites: one policy library with per-clinic versions and staff attestation records; consistent CPD-recordable AI-literacy activity with metadata-only completion evidence; a uniform human-review and receipt trail for AI-assisted outputs; per-clinic self-assessment snapshots and incident logs; and aggregated trust reporting per site. Tenant isolation is enforced at the database layer (row-level security, forced), so sites cannot see each other's records.

What it gives leadership is legibility: which sites have active policies, attestation coverage, learning activity, published client statements, and open incidents — as metadata, without clinical content ever entering the platform. It is governance infrastructure, not an EHR/PMS and not clinical decision support, and it makes no compliance guarantee for any site.

## 4. For an insurer / procurement reviewer

ANCHOR is a governance-evidence layer for veterinary practices using AI. For each practice it can show, as metadata only: an active AI-use policy and who attested to it; recorded AI-literacy activity; receipts demonstrating human review of AI-assisted outputs (with policy version and review decision, never content); a published client-transparency statement; incident/near-miss records with review workflow; and a self-assessment snapshot against governance readiness themes.

Three boundaries matter for your assessment: (1) evidence is metadata-only — ANCHOR holds no clinical records, so its trail complements rather than duplicates practice systems; (2) ANCHOR is aligned with emerging professional and regulatory expectations but is not certified, regulator-endorsed, or a compliance guarantee, and we will not represent otherwise; (3) the practice retains professional accountability — ANCHOR evidences governance practice, it does not underwrite outcomes.

## 5. For an AI vendor / provider discussion

ANCHOR is a practice-side governance layer, not a competitor and not a gatekeeper. We do not rate, validate, certify, accredit, or approve AI tools, and we do not intend to. What practices increasingly want to record is: what a tool is for, its limits, how data is handled, and what human oversight it assumes — and what remains undisclosed. A possible future ANCHOR layer would help practices *structure those questions and record the answers and gaps* (a structurer, not an arbiter). Providers with clear public documentation would find that dynamic favourable; nothing about it involves ANCHOR judging your product. Any such layer remains future work and is not commitment or announcement.
